1. SSPR Dynamic Shunt Dissipation & Duty Cycle Governing Law

In a Sequential Shunt Regulator managing $N$ solar array sections, the active bus voltage $V_{\text{bus}}$ is stabilized by keeping $k-1$ segments fully shorted (saturated $ON$), $N-k$ segments completely open ($OFF$), and exactly one segment modulated via Pulse-Width Modulation ($PWM$) at instantaneous duty ratio $D \in [0, 1]$.

$$P_{\text{diss}, i} = D_i \cdot I_{\text{sc}, i} \cdot V_{\text{sat}} + f_{\text{sw}} \cdot E_{\text{sw}}(V_{\text{bus}}, I_{\text{sc}, i}) + I_{\text{leak}} \cdot V_{\text{bus}}$$

Where $I_{\text{sc}, i}$ is the short-circuit current of array segment $i$, $V_{\text{sat}}$ is the forward collector-emitter / drain-source conduction drop, $f_{\text{sw}}$ is switching frequency (50 kHz), and $E_{\text{sw}}$ denotes the turn-on/turn-off transitional energy integral:

$$E_{\text{sw}} = \int_{0}^{t_{\text{rise}}} v_{ce}(t) i_c(t) dt + \int_{0}^{t_{\text{fall}}} v_{ce}(t) i_c(t) dt \approx \frac{1}{2} V_{\text{bus}} I_{\text{sc}} (t_r + t_f)$$
Parameter Limits: $V_{\text{bus}} = 100\,\text{V} \pm 1\%$, $I_{\text{sc}} = 20\,\text{A/channel}$, $t_r+t_f \le 38\,\text{ns}$.
2. Coupled Thermal Resistance Network & Junction Evolution

Heat transport from the semiconductor die through the beryllium oxide ($\text{BeO}$) substrate, copper heat spreader, and spacecraft radiator panel is governed by the multi-node thermal mass differential network:

$$C_{j} \frac{d T_{j, i}}{dt} = P_{\text{diss}, i} - \frac{T_{j, i} - T_{\text{case}, i}}{R_{\theta, jc}} - \sum_{m \ne i} \frac{T_{j, i} - T_{j, m}}{R_{\theta, im}}$$

Radiative heat rejection from the radiator skin ($A_{\text{rad}}$) into deep space background vacuum at $T_{\text{space}} = 3\,\text{K}$ enforces the nonlinear Stefan-Boltzmann boundary condition:

$$Q_{\text{rad}} = \epsilon \sigma A_{\text{rad}} \left( T_{\text{plate}}^4 - T_{\text{space}}^4 \right)$$
Coupling: $\epsilon = 0.88$ (silverized teflon tape), $R_{\theta, jc} = 0.42\,\text{K/W}$, $C_j = 0.85\,\text{J/K}$.
3. Collector-Emitter Pre-Breakdown Microscopic Physics

Thermal runaway initiates via positive feedback in minority carrier generation. As localized lattice temperature rises due to solder voiding or gate oxide micro-fractures, reverse leakage current $I_{\text{leak}}$ escalates exponentially according to Arrhenius activation:

$$I_{\text{leak}}(T) = I_0 \cdot \exp\left( -\frac{E_g - \Delta E_t}{k_B T} \right) \left[ 1 + \left(\frac{V_{\text{ce}}}{V_{\text{br}}}\right)^n \right]$$

When current crowding constricts the conductive channel cross section $A_{\text{eff}}$, current density $J = I/A_{\text{eff}}$ triggers localized impact ionization ($M \to \infty$), causing destructive thermal filamentation if the sector remains modulated in the PWM state.

$$\frac{\partial P_{\text{diss}}}{\partial T} > \frac{1}{R_{\theta, \text{total}}} \implies \text{Thermal Runaway Boundary Condition}$$
Instability Criterion: If $\partial P/\partial T > 1/R_{\theta}$, transistor junction destroys itself in $< 180\,\text{ms}$.
4. Diagnostic Inversion Observer & Resequencing Matrix

The SolarShunt real-time observer evaluates the error residual vector $\mathbf{r}(t)$ between measured thermistor temperatures $\mathbf{T}_{\text{meas}}$ and the model-predicted state vector $\mathbf{T}_{\text{pred}}(\mathbf{D}, P_{\text{excess}})$:

$$\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{T}_{\text{meas}}(t) - \left[ \mathbf{\Phi} \mathbf{T}(t-1) + \mathbf{\Gamma} \mathbf{P}_{\text{diss}}(\mathbf{D}) \right]$$

Upon detection of residual anomaly $r_k > \theta_{\text{crit}}$, an affine permutation operator $\mathbf{\Pi}_{\text{reseq}}$ shifts the active PWM modulation role from sector $k$ to the coolest adjacent sector $j = \arg\min_m(T_{j, m})$, immediately placing degraded leg $k$ into purely open or quiescent state:

$$\mathbf{D}_{\text{new}} = \mathbf{\Pi}_{\text{reseq}} \cdot \mathbf{D}_{\text{old}}, \quad \text{where } \mathbf{\Pi}_{k, k} = 0, \;\; \mathbf{\Pi}_{j, k} = 1$$
Observer Latency: $\tau_{\text{det}} = 12\,\text{ms}$, Switching Command Handshake: $< 40\,\mu\text{s}$.
